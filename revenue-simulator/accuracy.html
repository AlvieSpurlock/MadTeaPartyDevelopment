▮▮▮ Accuracy and limits

Exact arithmetic. Unproven forecasts.

The arithmetic is exact and checkable. Any forecast built on it is only as good as your inputs, and those inputs are assumptions, not measured facts.

Arithmetic Accuracy: Checkable

The same inputs always give the same outputs. You can verify the core by hand with one cohort:

DayRetentionActive usersRevenue that day

One cohort: 2,500 free launch installs, no paid UA, $0.085 ARPDAU, the default retention curve, every realism setting switched off. Active users × $0.085 = revenue.

How the settings stack.

With the starting realism settings on, day 0 becomes 2,500 × $0.085 × 70% warm-up × 95% (after the 5% revenue cut) = $141.31.

These figures match the app's own calculation, checked during development. No independent audit has been done.

Forecast Accuracy: Unproven

Not compared with real games.

The app hasn't been compared with the real results of live games. The starting values for retention, ARPDAU, CPI, revenue cut, running cost, warm-up, CPI scaling and paid-install retention are rough, deliberately modest estimates, not measured data and not benchmarks. The built-in holiday seasonality shape is illustrative only, and the currency rates are placeholders. Never treat output from the starting values as expected results.

What the Model Leaves Out

  • In-app purchases and subscriptions
  • Taxes beyond a flat rate on total profit, with no loss carry-forward; no other business or platform costs unless you enter them as running or dev cost
  • Separate retention for individual campaigns, markets or channels inside one game
  • Live exchange rates and currency risk
  • Cross-promo beyond an even split between launched games, and no limit on how many installs a game can absorb
  • Anything random: no variance, ranges or confidence intervals. Compare scenarios instead

How to Use It Responsibly

Run three cases

Low, base and high: duplicate the game tab and change retention and ARPDAU.

Use your own numbers

Replace every starting value with your soft-launch numbers as soon as you have them.

Read ranges

Compare scenarios with each other. Never quote a single run as a forecast.
