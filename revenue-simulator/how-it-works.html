▮▮▮ How it works

A short chain of arithmetic

No random numbers, no hidden adjustments: the same inputs always give the same outputs.

The Steps, in Plain Words

  1. Every install wave (launch, recurring free, paid, cross-promo) becomes a cohort that starts on its install day at 100% active.
  2. Each cohort shrinks along the retention curve. Free cohorts use the main curve; paid cohorts use the paid curve if you turned it on. Between your eight points the curve is smoothed on a log scale; after day 180 it stays flat.
  3. Daily active users on any day is the sum of every cohort's size times its retention at that cohort's age.
  4. Revenue per user on a day is your ARPDAU, adjusted for ad-network warm-up, monthly drift and that calendar month's seasonality. Net revenue is DAU times that, minus your revenue cut.
  5. Every 30 days after a game launches, any recurring free installs arrive, and the game spends its reinvestment share of the previous 30 days' net revenue on a new paid cohort: the push percentage for the first N months, then the sustain percentage.
  6. The cost per install of a paid wave is the market CPI, adjusted for that month's seasonality and for the size of the wave (CPI scaling). Installs = budget ÷ that CPI.
  7. Cross-promo runs in passes. First every game is simulated alone. Then, at each 30-day mark, a share of each game's average DAU over the previous 30 days is split evenly across the other launched games as free installs, and every game is simulated again. With chaining on, this repeats six times so received installs also promote the others; the effect fades quickly, and in testing six passes matched fifteen.
  8. Costs: the paid spend, the one-time dev cost on launch day, and the running cost spread evenly across each 30 days from launch. Profit before tax is net revenue minus all of them. Tax is your flat rate times total profit, if positive.
  9. The monthly take-home table subtracts from each 30-day period's net revenue the money committed from it to the next paid wave (and the first wave's upfront spend in month 1), plus that period's dev and running costs. The last period shows no commitment because the window ends before the next wave would be bought, so it often looks higher.

The Equations

DAU(d) = sum over cohorts c of N_c × R(d − s_c) Revenue per user = ARPDAU × warm-up(d) × (1 + drift)^((d − launch) / 30) × season(month) Net revenue(d) = DAU(d) × Revenue per user × (1 − revenue cut) Paid budget = reinvestment % × net revenue of the previous 30 days Paid installs = budget ÷ CPI_wave CPI_wave = market CPI × season_cpi(month) × (1 + k × log2(budget ÷ reference)) [only when budget > reference] Cross-promo i→j = rate_i × average DAU_i over the previous 30 days ÷ (games − 1) Profit = total net revenue − paid spend − dev cost − running costs Tax = tax rate × max(0, profit)

N is cohort size, s is its start day, R is the retention curve, k is the CPI rise per doubling.
